**1. Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2** (@Anthropic) — One underlying model, two access paths: Fable 5.2 is available to everyone, Mythos 5.2 is restricted to verified cybersecurity and life-science organizations. **2. Meta's Muse Ember: Haiku-level smarts at a third of the cost** (@AIatMeta) — Meta's smallest model ($0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, 1M context, 512K output) is built for the repetitive agent layer — tool calls, routing, extraction, coding subtasks — and matches Claude Haiku 5.5's best Artificial Analysis score (36) at about $0.14 per task vs $0.41. The catch: weights stay private, the per-task costs are Meta's list-pricing estimates, and knowledge is its weak spot — it declines 64% of trivia-style questions. *Why it's worth your time: The subagent layer just got cheap enough to run everywhere — pair a big planner with Ember workers.* [https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember)

**3. Google adds TPU 8tx and 8i Max — four TPU systems in one year** (Google) — TPU 8tx targets confidential inference (8t silicon with AMD EPYC hosts, data and weights isolated while in use) and dual-chip TPU 8i Max doubles the compute, SRAM, and HBM bandwidth of 8i for massive context windows. But Google published no price, no general-availability date, and no independent benchmarks — and both are repackagings of existing silicon, not new chips. *Why it's worth your time: Confidential inference and giant context windows are now first-class hardware targets — the shape of infra to watch if you run agents on sensitive data.* [https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/?ref=testingcatalog.com](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/?ref=testingcatalog.com)

**4. NVIDIA's GR00T 2 learns robot skills from human video** (@nvidia) — The open world action model, trained on 100,000 hours of egocentric human video plus robot data, hits 71% one-shot success across 50 unseen tasks and 95% with five demos, while Isaac Lab 3.0 with Newton claims up to 2.5x training throughput. The catch: code doesn't land until December, there's no public release date or license for the weights, and partner deployments at Foxconn and Siemens are testing and evaluation only. *Why it's worth your time: Few-shot robot skills learned from human video is the fastest path from demo to factory floor — if it ships as promised.* [https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/robotics-simulation-advancements-corl/](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/robotics-simulation-advancements-corl/)
